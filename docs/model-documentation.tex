\section{Scope}

SolarGen forecasts rooftop photovoltaic generation for a 10 kWp south-facing rooftop array in OHZ / Osterholz-Scharmbeck. The production forecast shown in the browser and local history app is an equal hourly blend of an Open-Meteo current physical forecast and a DWD stable forecast, capped at the observed 6.1 kWh hourly curtailment level.

The browser app obtains DWD data through the Open-Meteo DWD ICON endpoint. The local history app obtains DWD data from DWD MOSMIX. Both surfaces use the same transfer structure and final production blend.

\section{Source Physical Model}

For each source \(s \in \{\mathrm{OM}, \mathrm{DWD}\}\), hourly meteorological inputs are:

\[
I_{s,h},\ C_{s,h},\ R_{s,h},\ T_{s,h},
\]

where \(I\) is global tilted irradiance in \(\mathrm{W/m^2}\), \(C\) is cloud cover, \(R\) is precipitation, and \(T\) is ambient temperature.

The source physical model computes a rooftop generation curve:

\[
G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{s,h}
=
f_{\mathrm{site}}\left(I_{s,h}, C_{s,h}, R_{s,h}, T_{s,h}\right).
\]

The daily current total for source \(s\) is:

\[
G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{s}
=
\sum_{h=0}^{23} G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{s,h}.
\]

\section{DWD Stable Transfer}

The DWD current physical model alone did not generalize well. SolarGen therefore transforms DWD through a stable daily transfer model before production blending.

First compute a DWD sunshine/rain daily model:

\[
G^{\mathrm{raw}}_{\mathrm{DWD}}
=
\max\left(0,\ 18.3545 + 2.3510 H_{\mathrm{DWD}} - 1.9219 R^{+}_{\mathrm{DWD}}\right),
\]

where \(H_{\mathrm{DWD}}\) is DWD sunshine duration in hours and \(R^{+}_{\mathrm{DWD}}\) is DWD precipitation summed only over hours with positive tilted irradiance:

\[
R^{+}_{\mathrm{DWD}}
=
\sum_{h=0}^{23}
\mathbf{1}\{I_{\mathrm{DWD},h} > 0\} R_{\mathrm{DWD},h}.
\]

The stable DWD daily forecast is:

\[
\boxed{
G^{\mathrm{stable}}_{\mathrm{DWD}}
=
0.25 G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{\mathrm{DWD}}
+ 0.75 G^{\mathrm{raw}}_{\mathrm{DWD}}
+ 4.039
}
\]

\section{Production Blend}

There is no production-bias term or fitted production coefficient. The uncapped hourly blend is defined below, then capped at 6.1 kWh to obtain forecastable production. Uncapped output is retained as theoretical potential.

\section{Hourly Allocation}

The DWD current hourly curve is scaled to the stable DWD daily total:

\[
\widetilde{G}_{\mathrm{DWD},h}
=
G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{\mathrm{DWD},h}
\cdot
\frac{G^{\mathrm{stable}}_{\mathrm{DWD}}}
{\sum_{j=0}^{23}G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{\mathrm{DWD},j}}.
\]

The uncapped production hourly curve is:

\[
G^{\mathrm{uncap}}_h
=
0.50G^{\mathrm{cur}}_{\mathrm{OM},h}
+ 0.50\widetilde{G}_{\mathrm{DWD},h}.
\]

The curve is scaled by a small numerical factor if needed so that its uncapped total equals the daily source blend. Forecastable hourly and daily production are:

\[
G^{\mathrm{prod}}_h = \min(6.1, G^{\mathrm{uncap}}_h),
\qquad
G^{\mathrm{prod}} = \sum_{h=0}^{23}G^{\mathrm{prod}}_h.
\]

\section{Curtailment, Load, Battery, And Value}

The browser app applies the household load, battery, export, and value model to \(G^{\mathrm{prod}}_h\). For each hour:

\[
G^{\mathrm{deliv}}_h = \min(G^{\mathrm{prod}}_h, F),
\]

\[
G^{\mathrm{curt}}_h = G^{\mathrm{uncap}}_h - G^{\mathrm{deliv}}_h,
\]

where \(F=6\ \mathrm{kW}\) by default. Delivered PV first covers same-hour household load, then charges the battery, and remaining surplus is exported.

\section{Selection Evidence}

The production model was selected on all 129 paired Open-Meteo and DWD day-ahead dates with actuals from 2026-05-14 through 2026-09-25. The validation target caps hourly actual generation at 6.1 kWh so above-curtailment production from coincident household consumption does not drive the fit.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Model & In-sample MAE & LOO MAE & Rolling MAE & Comment \\
\midrule
Huber capped weather & 4.910 & 5.243 & 5.736 & 12 params \\
Hourly-capped equal blend & 5.313 & 5.313 & 5.736 & selected \\
Linear OM+DWD+bias & 5.297 & 5.418 & 5.748 & 3 params \\
Previous uncapped equal & 5.455 & 5.455 & 5.867 & previous \\
Old 0.73 blend & 5.541 & 5.541 & 5.985 & older \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The selected model is not the best in-sample or leave-one-out model. Huber regression improves leave-one-out MAE by only 0.070 kWh/day and ties the selected model on chronological rolling MAE while using 12 fitted degrees. The selection rule chooses the fewest fitted parameters within one standard error of the best rolling MAE.

Relative to the previous uncapped equal blend, the selected model improves aggregate leave-one-out MAE from 5.455 to 5.313 kWh and rolling RMSE from 8.529 to 8.025 kWh. The paired bootstrap 95 percent interval for MAE improvement includes zero, and June regresses by 0.504 kWh/day; the gain is therefore modest rather than decisive.
